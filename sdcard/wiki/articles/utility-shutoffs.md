Everyone in the home should know where these are and how they work. Find them **today**, not in an emergency.

## Water
- The main valve is usually where the water line enters the house: a basement, a crawl space, a utility room, by the water heater, or in a box near the street.
- A wheel handle turns **clockwise** to close. A lever handle closes when it is **crossways** to the pipe.
- Also find the small shutoffs under sinks and behind toilets.
- Close the main for a burst pipe, or if told your water is contaminated. See [Burst and Frozen Pipes](burst-pipe.md).

## Electricity
- The breaker panel is usually in a garage, basement, utility room or hallway. Know where the **main breaker** is.
- Turn off individual breakers first, then the main last.
- **If the panel is wet, or you are standing in water, do not touch it.** Call the utility and an electrician.
- After a flood or fire, do not restore power until it has been inspected.

## Gas
- The meter has a main valve. A quarter turn so the handle is **crossways** to the pipe shuts it off. You may need a wrench.
- Shut it off only if you smell gas or are told to, and you can reach it safely. **Only the gas company should turn it back on.** See [Gas Leak](gas-leak.md).
- Propane: close the tank valve clockwise.

## Get ready
- Keep a **wrench** or valve key near the valve, and tie it to the pipe.
- **Label** each shutoff with a tag. Mark the breaker panel too.
- Show everyone in the family, including older children. Take a photo of each and keep it in your go-bag.
- Look at the valve once a year and turn it a bit to make sure it still moves.
