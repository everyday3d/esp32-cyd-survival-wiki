Most snakebites are not fatal, especially with calm, correct first aid and medical care.

## Do
1. Move away from the snake. Do not try to catch or kill it; a photo from a distance is enough.
2. Keep the person calm and as still as possible.
3. Remove rings, watches and tight clothing from the bitten limb before swelling starts.
4. Keep the bitten limb still and at about heart level. Splint it if you can.
5. Mark the edge of swelling with a pen every 30 minutes, with the time.
6. Get to medical care as fast as safely possible. Carrying the person is better than letting them walk, if practical.

## Do not
- Cut the wound or try to suck out venom
- Use a tourniquet
- Apply ice or heat
- Give alcohol or medicines like aspirin
- Use electric shock or "snakebite kits"

## Regional note
In **Australia**, and for some snakes elsewhere (such as coral snakes, cobras and mambas), a **pressure immobilization bandage** is advised: wrap a wide elastic bandage firmly from the bite up the whole limb, then splint. Do **not** use this for North American rattlesnakes and other pit vipers, where it can increase tissue damage.

> Signs needing urgent help: trouble breathing or swallowing, drooping eyelids, bleeding gums, vomiting, fainting, rapidly spreading swelling.
