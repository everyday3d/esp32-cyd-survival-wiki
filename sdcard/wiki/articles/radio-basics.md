When phones fail, radio often still works. Each type has a different job.

## Radios that only listen
- **AM/FM radio:** local news and official instructions. A battery or hand-crank model is the first thing to own.
- **NOAA weather radio:** automatic weather and hazard alerts. See [NOAA Weather Radio and Alerts](noaa-weather-radio.md).
- **Shortwave:** news from far away, useful if local stations are down.

## Radios that talk
| Type | Range (typical) | License (US) | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FRS** walkie-talkie | 1 to 3 km (0.5 to 2 mi) | none | family, neighbors |
| **GMRS** | 3 to 8 km (2 to 5 mi), more with repeaters | license, no exam | families, neighborhoods, vehicles |
| **MURS** | 2 to 5 km (1 to 3 mi) | none | small groups, farms |
| **CB** | 5 to 15 km (3 to 10 mi) | none | highways, trucks |
| **Ham (amateur)** handheld | 5 to 50 km (3 to 30+ mi), with repeaters | license with exam | wide-area help, nets |
| **Mesh (this device)** | a few hundred meters per hop | none | text between boards |

Range is shorter in hills, forests and buildings. See [Getting More Range](radio-range.md).

## What to keep
- Two or more matching **FRS/GMRS handhelds** for family and neighbors. See [FRS and GMRS Walkie-Talkies](frs-gmrs.md).
- A **battery or hand-crank AM/FM/NOAA radio.**
- Spare batteries, or a power bank and cable. Rechargeable handhelds need a way to charge.
- A **ham handheld** if you or a neighbor is licensed. See [Ham Radio in an Emergency](ham-radio.md).
- Pen and paper. Write down frequencies, call signs and messages.

## Licenses
Rules differ by country. In the US, FRS, MURS and CB need no license. GMRS needs a simple license with no exam, and amateur radio needs an exam. Learn and practice **before** a disaster. In a real, life-threatening emergency with no other way to get help, calling for help on any radio you have is widely accepted.

## Good habits
- Test radios monthly. Charge them. Keep them in a bag with spare batteries.
- Agree on a **channel and a check-in time** with your family before you need them.
- Keep transmissions short to save batteries and keep the channel free for others.
