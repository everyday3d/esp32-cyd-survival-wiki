Older adults, people with disabilities and people with medical conditions face more risk in a disaster and often need help earlier. Plan with them.

## Make a medical sheet
Keep a **one-page summary** in a waterproof bag and in the go-bag:
- Name, age, photo, and emergency contacts
- Conditions, allergies and implants
- **All medicines**, with doses and the pharmacy phone number
- Doctor names and numbers
- Insurance and ID details
- Communication needs (hearing, vision, language) and any service animal

## Medicines and supplies
- Keep **at least 7 days** of medicine, and 2 weeks if you can. Rotate it so it does not expire.
- Keep **insulin cold:** a cooler with ice packs or a special pouch.
- Spare glasses, hearing aid batteries, a cane or walker, and dentures.
- Incontinence supplies, wipes and bags.
- A list of supply sources and spare chargers for devices.

## Power-dependent equipment
- **Oxygen concentrators, ventilators and powered beds** need backup power. Talk to your supplier and the power company, and register as a medical-need customer.
- Keep a **backup tank** of oxygen and know how many hours it lasts. No flames or smoking near oxygen.
- Know the nearest hospital or shelter with power, and plan how to get there before an outage.
- See [Generator Safety](generator-safety.md) and [Carbon Monoxide](carbon-monoxide.md).

## Getting out
- Plan evacuation routes and **who will help**. Do not leave this to chance.
- Ask neighbors, family or a local agency to check on you early. Share your plan and keys.
- If someone cannot use stairs, identify a ground-floor safe room or wheelchair-friendly exit.
- Practice moving a wheelchair up and down stairs with helpers. Do not try to carry someone without training unless it is life-threatening.

## Communication
- Keep a pen and paper, a communication board or a phone with text. Tell responders if you are deaf, blind or non-verbal.
- For people with dementia or autism, keep **routines, familiar items and calm** as much as possible, and carry ID. See [Helping Kids Cope](kids-calm.md) and [Lost or Missing Child](lost-child.md).

## Check on neighbors
Call or visit older neighbors and people who live alone **before and after** a disaster. Many deaths happen in the days after, in heat, cold and outages. See [Extreme Heat Waves](extreme-heat.md) and [Blizzards and Ice Storms](blizzard.md).
