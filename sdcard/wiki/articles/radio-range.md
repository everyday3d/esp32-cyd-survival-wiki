Most handheld radios use line-of-sight signals. If you can see the other person, you can usually reach them. Anything in the way weakens the signal.

## Get high
- **Height is the best range booster.** Move to a hilltop, a rooftop, an upper floor or the top of a ridge.
- A few meters of height can double your range.
- Move out of a valley, a ravine or a basement.

## Clear the path
- Step away from buildings, vehicles, dense trees and cliffs.
- Go to a window, or outdoors, when inside a building.
- Metal, concrete and water absorb signals. A car roof can help as a reflector, but being inside a car blocks it a little.

## Hold the radio right
- Keep the antenna **straight up**, not tilted or pressed against your head or body.
- Keep your hand off the antenna. Do not hold it near your mouth.
- Take the radio out of a pocket or pack while talking.

## Better antennas
- A longer, replacement antenna (often sold for handhelds) can improve range a bit.
- A magnet-mount or roof antenna on a car, connected to a car radio, is much better than a handheld.
- Do not transmit with no antenna attached. It can damage the radio.

## Relay
- A third person on high ground can **relay** messages between two who cannot hear each other.
- In ham radio, repeaters do this automatically. See [Ham Radio in an Emergency](ham-radio.md).
- On this device, every board you add extends the mesh. See [Using the Mesh Between Boards](mesh-messaging.md).

## Power and batteries
- Cold batteries lose power quickly. Keep radios and spare batteries inside your coat.
- Use low power for close calls. Switch to high power only when you need it.
- Turn the radio off between calls, or use a scheduled check-in, to save the battery.

## Test before you need it
Try your radios at known distances and in different places. Write down what works.
