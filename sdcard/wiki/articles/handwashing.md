## When to wash
- After using the toilet or changing a diaper
- Before preparing or eating food
- After handling raw meat, trash, animals or someone who is sick
- After treating a wound or caring for the sick

## The method (about 20 seconds)
1. Wet your hands. Pour a little water over them, away from the clean supply.
2. Lather with soap. Scrub palms, backs, between fingers and under nails for **20 seconds**.
3. Rinse with a small pour. Dry on a clean cloth or let the air dry them.

**Soap and a quart of water is plenty.** Soap does most of the work, not the amount of water.

## With very little water
- **Tippy tap:** hang a jug from a string with a small hole near the cap. Tip it with a foot-string for a thin stream. Keep the soap on a string beside it.
- Use a spray bottle or a cup with a hole poked in it.
- Wash over a bucket and reuse the grey water to flush a toilet or water plants. Do not use it for drinking.

## Hand sanitizer
- Use one with **at least 60% alcohol**. Rub on all surfaces until dry (about 20 seconds).
- It does not work well on visibly dirty or greasy hands. Wipe off dirt first.
- It does not remove some germs, such as norovirus and C. diff. Soap and water is better after the toilet.

## No soap
- Use ash or clean sand to scrub, then rinse. It is better than water alone.
- Scrub well and rinse with clean water.

> Don't use bleach or harsh cleaners on skin. Save diluted bleach for surfaces.
