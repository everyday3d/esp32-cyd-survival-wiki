## Why 72 hours
Help may take three days or more after a big storm, quake or outage. A kit covers the gap. Keep it in one place, in a bag or bin you can carry.

## Water and food (per person)
- **Water:** 4 L (1 gallon) per day, so 12 L for three days. Add purification tablets or a filter.
- Food that needs no cooking: canned goods with a manual opener, protein bars, nuts, dried fruit, crackers, peanut butter.
- Baby food, formula and pet food if needed.

## Light, power and communication
- Flashlight or headlamp and spare batteries
- Battery or hand-crank radio (NOAA weather radio if you can)
- **Power bank, charging cables and this device**
- Whistle, and a printed list of key phone numbers
- Cash in small bills

## First aid and health
- First aid kit, any prescriptions for a week or more, glasses, a copy of the medication list
- Pain reliever, antihistamine, anti-diarrheal, sunscreen, insect repellent
- Masks, gloves and hand sanitizer

## Shelter and warmth
- Warm clothes, a change of clothes, sturdy shoes, rain gear
- Emergency blankets or sleeping bags, a tarp, and rope
- Matches or lighters in a waterproof bag

## Tools and hygiene
- Multi-tool or knife, duct tape, work gloves
- Trash bags, toilet paper, soap, wipes, feminine products, toothbrush
- Garbage bags and a bucket for an emergency toilet

## Papers
- Copies of ID, insurance, deeds and bank info in a waterproof bag, plus a USB copy.
- Local maps and your family plan.

## Keep it fresh
- Check the kit **twice a year**. Swap food, water, batteries and medicines before they expire.
- Update sizes of kids' clothes.
- Keep a smaller kit in each car.

> Start with water, food, light and a radio. You can add the rest over time.
