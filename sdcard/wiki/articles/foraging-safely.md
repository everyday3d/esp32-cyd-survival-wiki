> Only eat a plant if you can identify it with **100% certainty**. Several deadly plants look like common edible ones.

## Never eat
- **Wild mushrooms**, unless you are an expert. Some deadly species look harmless, and symptoms can take a day to appear, too late to treat.
- Plants that look like **wild carrot, parsnip or parsley** (white umbrella-shaped flower clusters). This family includes water hemlock and poison hemlock, among the deadliest plants anywhere.
- Plants with milky or discolored sap (some exceptions exist, but avoid unless you know them).
- Beans, bulbs or seeds inside pods, unless known.
- Plants with an almond smell (can mean cyanide).
- White and yellow berries (most are poisonous); about half of red berries are too.

## Commonly known safe foods (where you know them)
- **Cattail:** young shoots, roots and pollen. Found in marshes.
- **Dandelion:** leaves, flowers and roots.
- **Clover:** leaves and flowers, best cooked.
- **Acorns:** must be shelled, crushed and soaked in several changes of water to leach out bitter tannins.
- **Pine:** inner bark and needles (tea). Avoid yew, which looks similar and is very toxic.
- **Blackberries, raspberries, blueberries:** aggregate and blue berries are among the safest.

## The universal edibility test
Military manuals describe a slow test (touching, holding in the mouth, then eating small amounts over 24+ hours). It takes a long time, does not detect every toxin, and **does not work for mushrooms**. Use it only in true long-term emergencies with no other option.

## Cooking helps
Boiling breaks down some toxins and kills parasites. Throw away the water if a plant is bitter.
