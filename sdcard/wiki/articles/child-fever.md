A fever is the body fighting an infection. How the child **acts** matters more than the number on the thermometer.

## When it is an emergency
- **A baby under 3 months with a temperature of 38 C (100.4 F) or higher.** Get help right away, even if the baby looks well.
- A baby 3 to 12 months with a fever of 39 C (102.2 F) or more, or any fever that lasts more than a day or two.
- A child with fever **and** any of these:
  - Trouble breathing, or fast breathing that does not settle
  - A stiff neck, severe headache, or sensitivity to light
  - A rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass
  - Very sleepy, hard to wake, confused, or very floppy
  - A seizure. See [Seizures](seizures.md).
  - Signs of dehydration: no tears, dry mouth, no urine for 8 hours, sunken soft spot on a baby's head
  - A fever that lasts more than 3 days, or is above 40 C (104 F)

## How to measure
- Use a digital thermometer. Under the arm is easy but less exact. For a baby, a rectal reading is the most accurate.
- Do not use a glass mercury thermometer.

## What to do at home
1. **Offer fluids often.** Small sips, or breast milk and formula for babies. See [Diarrhea, Vomiting and Rehydration](diarrhea-vomiting.md).
2. Dress them lightly and keep the room comfortable. Do not bundle them up.
3. Let them rest.
4. **Medicine is for comfort, not to bring the number to normal.** Use acetaminophen (paracetamol), or ibuprofen only if the child is older than 6 months. Follow the label by **weight**, use the dosing syringe, and do not give extra doses.
5. **Never give aspirin.**
6. A lukewarm sponge bath can help comfort. Do not use cold water, ice or alcohol rubs.

## Febrile seizures
Some young children have a short seizure with a fast-rising fever. It looks frightening, but is usually brief and harmless.
- Lay the child on their side on a soft surface and clear the area. Do not put anything in their mouth or hold them down.
- Time it. **Call for help if it lasts more than 5 minutes**, the child does not wake up afterward, or it is their first one.
