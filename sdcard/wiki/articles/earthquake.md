## During the shaking: Drop, Cover, Hold On
1. **Drop** to your hands and knees right where you are.
2. **Cover** your head and neck with your arms. Get under a sturdy table or desk if one is within a few steps. If not, crawl next to an interior wall away from windows.
3. **Hold on** to your shelter and move with it until the shaking stops.

- **Stay where you are.** Most injuries happen when people run or try to leave a building.
- **In bed:** stay there and cover your head with a pillow.
- **Do not stand in a doorway.** It is no stronger than other parts of a house.
- **Outside:** move to open space away from buildings, trees, streetlights and power lines, then drop, cover and hold on.
- **In a car:** pull over in a clear area, away from bridges, overpasses and wires. Stay inside with your seat belt on until the shaking stops.
- **Do not use elevators.**

## Coastal areas
If the shaking is strong or lasts a long time, or you are told to, **go to high ground or inland at once**. A tsunami can arrive within minutes. Do not wait for an official warning.

## After the shaking
- Expect aftershocks. Drop, cover and hold on again each time.
- Check yourself and others for injuries. See [First Aid Priorities](first-aid-priorities.md).
- Put on sturdy shoes and watch for broken glass.
- **If you smell gas or hear hissing, get out and call from outside.** See [Gas Leak](gas-leak.md). Do not light matches or flip switches.
- Check for fires and put out small ones. See [Fire Safety](fire-safety.md).
- Check the building for cracks and damage. Leave if it looks unsafe.
- Use text messages instead of calls.

## If you are trapped
- Do not light a match. Do not move around, which kicks up dust.
- Cover your mouth with cloth.
- Tap on a pipe or wall, or use a whistle, so rescuers can find you. Shout only as a last resort, because it makes you inhale dust.
