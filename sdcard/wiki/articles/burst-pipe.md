A burst pipe can pour out hundreds of liters of water an hour and ruin a house in a day.

## If a pipe bursts
1. **Shut off the main water valve** right away. See [Utility Shutoffs](utility-shutoffs.md).
2. **Open faucets** (cold and hot) at the lowest and highest points to drain what is left in the pipes.
3. **Turn off the electricity** to wet areas if you can do it safely from a dry spot at the panel. **Do not step into standing water near outlets or appliances.**
4. Turn off the water heater (power or gas) so it does not run dry and burn out.
5. Move belongings out of the water and mop up. Use fans and open windows to dry the area quickly.
6. Take photos for insurance, and call a plumber.

## Frozen pipes (water not coming out)
- Open the faucet so melting water can flow.
- Warm the pipe slowly with a **hair dryer**, a heating pad or warm towels. Start near the faucet and work back.
- **Never use an open flame, a propane torch or a kerosene heater.** They can start a fire or burst the pipe.
- If you cannot find the frozen section, or the pipe is already cracked, shut off the water and call a plumber.
- Keep the faucet open until full flow returns.

## Prevent freezing
- Keep the house at no lower than 13 C (55 F) even when away.
- In a hard freeze, let a faucet **drip** and open cabinet doors under sinks.
- Insulate pipes in garages, crawl spaces and outside walls.
- Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses and shut off outdoor spigots in fall.
- If the power may fail for long, drain the pipes: shut off the main, open all faucets and flush toilets.

## After
Water-damaged materials can grow mold in 24 to 48 hours. Dry things out fast and throw out soaked insulation and carpets.
