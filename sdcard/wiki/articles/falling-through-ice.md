## Is the ice safe?
Clear, hard, new ice is strongest. White or cloudy ice is weaker, often about half as strong.
- Under 5 cm (2 in): **stay off**.
- 10 cm (4 in) of clear ice: walking for one person.
- 13-18 cm (5-7 in): a snowmobile or small all-terrain vehicle.
- 20-30 cm (8-12 in): a small car.

Ice is never the same thickness everywhere. It is thinner near inlets, outlets, moving water, docks, rocks, logs and dark spots, and under snow. Check thickness every few steps with an ice chisel, axe or auger.

> Never go onto ice alone. Carry ice picks (two spikes on a cord around your neck) and a throw rope.

## If you fall in: the 1-10-1 rule
![The 1-10-1 rule for falling through ice](img/ice_1101.jpg)

- **1 minute:** cold shock makes you gasp and breathe fast. Keep your head above water, do not panic, and slow your breathing. It passes.
- **10 minutes:** of useful movement before your arms and legs go numb. Use them to get out now.
- **1 hour:** before you lose consciousness from hypothermia, even if you cannot get out. Keep your arms on the ice; they may freeze to it and hold you up.

## Getting out
1. Turn toward the direction you came from. That ice held you before.
2. Put your arms flat on the ice. Use ice picks, keys or a knife to grip.
3. **Kick your legs hard** to bring your body up level with the surface, like a swimmer.
4. Pull and kick until your chest and hips are on the ice.
5. **Roll away** from the hole. Do not stand up until you are on thick ice.
6. Get to shelter and warmth immediately. See [Hypothermia and Frostbite](hypothermia.md).

## Helping someone else
**Reach, throw, don't go.** Do not run out to them; you will likely fall in too.
- Call for help.
- Reach with a long pole, branch, rope or ladder while lying flat or standing on solid ice or shore.
- Throw a rope or anything that floats.
- If you must go out, lie flat to spread your weight and push something ahead of you for them to grab.
