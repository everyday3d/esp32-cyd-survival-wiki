Most winter storm deaths come from car trouble, exposure, carbon monoxide and heart strain from shoveling.

## Before the storm
- Stock food, water, blankets, batteries and any medicines you need. See [Home Emergencies and Power Outages](power-outage.md).
- Fill the car with fuel and keep a winter kit in it. See [Car Emergency Kit](car-kit.md).
- Plan how you will keep one room warm if the power fails.

## During a blizzard
- **Stay inside and off the roads.** Whiteouts make it hard to see even a few feet.
- Heat one room. Close doors, stuff towels under them and hang blankets over windows.
- Dress in layers and keep a hat on. See [Cold Weather and Snow Shelters](cold-weather.md).
- Keep fluids up. Eat regularly, because your body burns food to make heat.
- **Never burn charcoal or run a generator or camp stove indoors.** See [Carbon Monoxide](carbon-monoxide.md).
- Use flashlights, not candles. If you must use a heater, keep it away from things that burn.

## Ice storms
- Ice downs trees and power lines. Stay away from windows, trees and lines.
- Walk with short, flat steps, feet turned out slightly, arms free.
- Do not drive on ice. Even 4-wheel drive does not help you stop.
- Use handrails where there are any, and carry a small bag of sand or cat litter to sprinkle on ice.

## Shoveling
- Heavy snow causes heart attacks. Push snow rather than lifting it, take breaks, and stop for chest pain, dizziness or shortness of breath.
- Keep vents for furnaces, dryers and stoves clear of snow so exhaust can escape.

## Frostbite and hypothermia
Watch for numb, white or waxy skin, and shivering, slurred speech or confusion. See [Hypothermia and Frostbite](hypothermia.md).
