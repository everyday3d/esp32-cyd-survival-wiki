A solar still uses the sun to draw moisture from soil and plants. It makes small amounts, but the water is distilled and clean.

![Cross-section of a solar still](img/solar_still.jpg)

## Building it
1. Dig a bowl-shaped hole about 90 cm (3 ft) wide and 60 cm (2 ft) deep, in a sunny spot with damp soil if possible.
2. Put a container in the center at the bottom.
3. Add green, non-poisonous plants around the container (not in it) to add moisture.
4. Cover the hole with clear plastic. Seal the edges with soil and rocks so no air escapes.
5. Put a small rock in the middle of the plastic so it forms a cone right above the container.
6. Water condenses on the plastic, runs down to the low point and drips into the container.

## Tips
- Use a drinking tube from the container to the outside so you do not need to open the still.
- You can pour salt water or urine into the pit (not the container). The still separates clean water.
- Build several stills if you rely on them.

> A still often yields well under a liter per day, and digging makes you sweat. In hot deserts, do the work at dawn or dusk, and only if there is no better source.
