Floods kill more people in vehicles than anywhere else. Water is stronger than it looks.

## Before the water comes
- Know the high ground near you and two ways to reach it.
- Move valuables and medicines up high. Keep a go-bag by the door.
- If a flood warning is issued for your area, **leave early**. Do not wait for water to reach your door.

## During a flood
- **Move to higher ground right away.** Do not wait for instructions if water is rising fast.
- **Turn around, do not drown.** Never walk or drive through floodwater.
  - 15 cm (6 in) of moving water can knock an adult down.
  - 30 cm (1 ft) can float many cars.
  - 60 cm (2 ft) can carry away most vehicles, trucks included.
- You cannot judge depth or what is under the surface. Roads may be washed out.
- If your car stalls in rising water, **leave it** and get to higher ground. See [Car in Water](car-in-water.md).
- Stay out of basements and low areas. Do not touch electrical equipment if you are wet or standing in water.
- Inside a building with rising water, go to the highest floor. Go on the roof only if you must, and signal for help. Do not climb into a closed attic you cannot get out of.

## After a flood
- Floodwater carries sewage, fuel and chemicals. Wash hands often and avoid contact with it.
- Treat all tap and well water as unsafe until officials say otherwise. See [Making Water Safe to Drink](purifying-water.md).
- Do not turn the power on if it has been under water. Get it checked first.
- Throw out food that touched floodwater, and cans that are swollen or dented.
- Dry the building out fast, within a day or two, to limit mold. Wear gloves and a mask for cleanup.
- Watch for snakes, sharp debris and damaged roads.
