In the wild, infection is a bigger long-term danger than most cuts.

## Cleaning
1. Stop the bleeding with pressure.
2. **Irrigate** with lots of drinkable water: at least 1 liter for a wound. Squirt it with force, for example from a bag with a pinhole or a syringe.
3. Remove dirt and splinters with clean tweezers.
4. Clean the skin around the wound with soap and water.

## Closing and covering
- Small, clean cuts can be closed with tape strips.
- Leave dirty wounds, bites and punctures **open**, covered with a clean dressing, so they can drain.
- Change dressings daily, or when wet or dirty.

## Signs of infection
- Redness and swelling that spreads
- Increasing pain, warmth and pus
- **Red streaks** moving up from the wound, fever or chills: this is serious. Get to medical care urgently.

If infected: open the wound if it was closed, soak in warm clean water several times a day, keep it clean and covered.

## Blisters
Cover hot spots early with tape. For large blisters, drain from the edge with a clean needle, leave the skin on, and pad around it.

> Animal bites can carry rabies. Wash any bite for 15 minutes with soap and water and get medical care as soon as possible.
