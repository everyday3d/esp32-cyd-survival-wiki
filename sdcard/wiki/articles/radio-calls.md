## Step 1: listen
Listen for a few seconds. If someone is already talking about an emergency, wait.

## Step 2: call for help
Press the transmit button, wait a second, then speak slowly and clearly:

> **"Mayday, mayday, mayday.** This is [your name or call sign]. [Repeat once.] I am at [location]. I have [what is wrong]. [Number of people]. I need [what you need]. **Over.**"

Example: *"Mayday, mayday, mayday. This is Alex. I am on Ridge Road, 2 km south of the old bridge. My car is in a ditch and one person has a broken leg. Two people. I need an ambulance. Over."*

Use **"Mayday"** only for danger to life. For less serious urgent calls say **"Pan-pan"** (pronounced "pahn-pahn") three times.

## Step 3: wait and repeat
- Release the button and listen. If nobody answers, wait about one minute, then try again.
- Try other channels or frequencies if you can. See [FRS and GMRS Walkie-Talkies](frs-gmrs.md) and [Ham Radio in an Emergency](ham-radio.md).
- Keep saying your location. Rescuers need it.

## Say these things
1. **Who** you are, and how many people.
2. **Where** you are (road, mile marker, landmark, coordinates).
3. **What** is wrong.
4. **What help** you need.

## Useful radio words
- **Over:** I am finished and waiting for your reply.
- **Out:** this conversation is finished. Do not say "over and out" together.
- **Roger:** I received your message.
- **Say again:** repeat your last message.
- **Stand by:** wait.
- **Affirmative / negative:** yes / no.

## Phonetic alphabet
Alfa, Bravo, Charlie, Delta, Echo, Foxtrot, Golf, Hotel, India, Juliett, Kilo, Lima, Mike, November, Oscar, Papa, Quebec, Romeo, Sierra, Tango, Uniform, Victor, Whiskey, X-ray, Yankee, Zulu.

Say numbers one at a time: "four five two" for 452. Say "niner" for 9 so it is not confused with "five".

## Tips
- Speak slowly, with the radio a few centimeters from your mouth.
- Keep messages short. Save the battery.
- After rescue, tell the net or channel that the emergency is over.
