A small kit you actually carry beats a big kit left at home. Build it around the Rule of 3s.

## Core items
- **Fire:** lighter, ferro rod, waterproof matches, tinder (cotton balls with petroleum jelly)
- **Shelter:** emergency blanket or bivy bag, small tarp, 15 m of cord
- **Water:** metal bottle you can boil in, purification tablets, filter
- **Light:** headlamp with spare batteries
- **Signal:** whistle, signal mirror, bright cloth
- **Tools:** fixed-blade or folding knife, duct tape
- **First aid:** bandages, gauze, tape, tourniquet, pain relievers, personal medicines
- **Navigation:** map of the area, compass
- **Food:** high-calorie bars

## Clothing
- Warm layer (wool or synthetic, not cotton)
- Rain jacket
- Hat and gloves
- Spare socks

> Cotton loses its warmth when wet. In cold or wet weather, wool and synthetics can be the difference between cold and hypothermic.

## Tell someone
The most important item is not in the kit: leave your route and expected return time with someone you trust.
