## Signs of a break
Pain, swelling, bruising, deformity, grating, or the person cannot use the limb. If in doubt, treat it as broken.

## Splinting
1. Control any bleeding first. Cover open wounds and bone ends with a clean dressing. Do not push bone back in.
2. **Check circulation, feeling and movement** beyond the injury (fingers or toes): warm, pink, can feel your touch, can wiggle.
3. Splint the limb in the position you find it, unless it is badly bent and there is no pulse beyond it (then gently straighten it once, along its length, if trained).
4. Immobilize the **joint above and below** a broken bone, or the bones above and below an injured joint.
5. Use anything rigid: sticks, trekking poles, rolled magazines, a sleeping pad. Pad well with clothing.
6. Tie in place firmly but not tightly, with knots away from the injury.
7. **Re-check circulation** after splinting and every 30 minutes. If fingers or toes go pale, cold or numb, loosen the ties.

## Slings
For arm, wrist and collarbone injuries, support the arm in a sling made from a triangle of cloth or a shirt, with the hand slightly higher than the elbow.

## Sprains
Rest, cool packs wrapped in cloth for 20 minutes at a time, gentle compression wrap, and raise the limb. A sprained ankle may be walkable with a firm wrap and a stick if you must move.

> Suspect a **spine injury** after a fall, crash or blow to the head with neck or back pain, numbness or tingling. Keep the head and neck still and do not move the person unless they are in danger.
