## Stop the burning
- If clothing is on fire: stop, drop and roll, or smother with a blanket.
- Remove rings, watches and tight clothing near the burn before it swells. Do not pull off clothing stuck to the skin.

## Cool it
- Cool with clean, cool running water for **20 minutes** (at least 10). This helps even if started late, up to about 3 hours after.
- Do **not** use ice, butter, oil, toothpaste or ointment on a fresh burn.
- Keep the rest of the person warm. Cooling a large burn can cause hypothermia, especially in children.

## Cover it
- Cover loosely with clean plastic wrap (laid on, not wrapped tight), a clean non-stick dressing, or a clean plastic bag over a hand.
- Do not pop blisters.

## Serious burns - get help
- Larger than the person's palm, or deep (white, leathery or charred)
- On the face, hands, feet, genitals or over joints
- Electrical or chemical burns
- Burns in the airway: soot around the nose or mouth, hoarse voice, coughing, trouble breathing. These can swell shut. **Urgent.**

## Chemical burns
Brush off dry chemicals, then flush with lots of water for at least 20 minutes.

> Large burns cause dangerous fluid loss. Watch for [Shock](shock.md) and give sips of fluid if the person is alert and help is far away.
