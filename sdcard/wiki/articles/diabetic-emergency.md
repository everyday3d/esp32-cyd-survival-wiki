## Low blood sugar (the common emergency)
**Signs:** shaky, sweating, pale, hungry, fast heartbeat, confused, cranky or acting drunk, then seizures or passing out.

**If they are awake and can swallow:**
1. Give **15-20 g of fast sugar**: 3-4 glucose tablets, half a cup (120 ml) of juice or regular soda, or a tablespoon of sugar or honey.
2. Wait 15 minutes and recheck. If still unwell, give the same again.
3. Once better, give a snack with starch and protein, such as crackers and peanut butter.

**If they are unconscious or cannot swallow:**
- **Do not put food or drink in the mouth.** Place them on their side and **call emergency services**.
- If they carry glucagon and you know how, give it.

## High blood sugar
- Signs: very thirsty, passing lots of urine, tired, nausea, fruity breath, deep fast breathing, drowsy.
- Give water if they are awake. Needs medical care, especially with vomiting or confusion.

## When you are not sure
- **Treat it as low blood sugar.** Sugar helps a low and does little harm in a high, while untreated lows are dangerous.

## In a long outage
- **Insulin** can be kept at room temperature (below about 25 C / 77 F) for about 28 days. Keep it out of sun and heat. Do not let it freeze.
- Keep sugar tablets handy and know the signs of a low. Eat regular meals and do not skip doses.
- Do not take their insulin or medicine unless you know what to do.

> A diabetic person who seems drunk, confused or odd should get sugar first and questions later.
