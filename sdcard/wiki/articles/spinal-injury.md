## Suspect a spinal injury if
- A fall from height, a diving or sports injury, a car crash, or a hard blow to the head or back
- Neck or back pain, tenderness or a deformity
- Numbness, tingling or weakness in arms or legs, or they cannot move them
- Loss of control of bladder or bowel
- They are unconscious and you do not know what happened

## What to do
1. **Tell them not to move.** Call emergency services.
2. **Keep the head, neck and back in line.** Kneel behind their head and hold it gently still with both hands, or place rolled towels or clothing on both sides of the head.
3. Do not bend, twist or lift. Do not remove a helmet unless it blocks breathing.
4. Check breathing. Keep them warm with a blanket.
5. Stay with them and talk calmly until help arrives.

## If they are not breathing
- **Airway first.** Open it by lifting the jaw forward without tilting the head. If that does not work, tilt the head back just enough to open the airway. Breathing matters more than the spine.
- Start CPR.

## If you must move them
Only move them for immediate danger such as fire, rising water or a collapsing structure, or to place an unconscious person on their side so they do not choke.
- Use at least 3 people. Roll them as one piece, keeping head, neck and back in a straight line (**log roll**).
- If you are alone, drag them by the shoulders or clothing along their length, never sideways.

> If someone is alone and far from help, think carefully. A person who can feel and move all four limbs and has no neck pain is less likely to have a serious injury, but when in doubt, keep them still.
