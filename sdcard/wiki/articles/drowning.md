## Rescue safely
1. **Shout for help and call emergency services** if you can.
2. **Reach or throw. Do not go in.** Drowning people grab and pull rescuers under.
3. Reach with a pole, branch, paddle or clothing. Throw a rope, a ring, a cooler, a jug or anything that floats.
4. Only swim out as a last resort, and only if you are a strong swimmer. Bring something that floats and keep it between you and them.

## Once they are out
1. Lay them on their back on firm ground. Check for breathing for no more than 10 seconds.
2. **Not breathing:** start with **5 rescue breaths**, then CPR (30 compressions, 2 breaths). Drowning is a lack of air, so breaths matter more than usual.
3. Keep going until they breathe, help takes over, or you cannot continue.
4. If they vomit, roll them onto their side, clear the mouth, then roll back and continue.

## If they are breathing
- Roll them onto their **side** (recovery position) and keep them warm. Wet clothes off, dry layers on.
- Stay with them and watch their breathing.

## Always get medical help
- Anyone who needed rescue, coughed a lot, or was under water, even if they seem fine now. Lungs can fill with fluid hours later.
- Watch for a cough that will not stop, fast breathing, blue lips, unusual sleepiness or confusion.

## Cold water
- Cold water drowning victims have been revived after a long time under. **Do not give up early.** Keep going with CPR and rewarm them.
- If someone is very cold, handle them gently and keep them flat.

> Suspect a neck or back injury after a dive or fall. Keep the head, neck and body in a line as you move them, unless they are not breathing.
