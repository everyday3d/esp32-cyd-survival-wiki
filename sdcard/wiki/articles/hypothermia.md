Hypothermia can happen well above freezing when someone is wet, tired and in the wind.

## Signs - watch for the "umbles"
- **Mild:** shivering, stumbling, fumbling hands, mumbling, poor decisions.
- **Moderate to severe:** shivering slows or stops, confusion, drowsiness, slurred speech, acting strangely (some people even undress).

## Treatment
1. Get them out of the wind and wet. Shelter first.
2. Replace wet clothes with dry ones, or cover wet clothes with a waterproof layer if you cannot change them.
3. Insulate from the ground, and wrap the whole body, including the head and neck.
4. Put warm (not hot) water bottles or heat packs, wrapped in cloth, on the chest, armpits and back.
5. If fully awake and able to swallow: give warm, sweet drinks and food.
6. Share body heat in a sleeping bag if needed.

> Handle a severely cold person very **gently** and keep them lying flat. Rough movement can trigger a fatal heart rhythm. Do not rub the skin. No alcohol.

If they are unresponsive and not breathing normally after checking for up to a full minute, start [CPR](cpr.md).

## Frostbite
- **Signs:** numb, white or grey-yellow skin that feels hard or waxy. Fingers, toes, nose, ears.
- Do not rub it, and do not use direct heat like a fire or stove.
- **Only rewarm if it will not freeze again.** Refreezing does much more damage.
- Rewarm in warm water about 37-39 C (99-102 F), comfortable to your own hand, for 15-30 minutes. It will be very painful.
- Do not pop blisters. Cover loosely with dry, clean dressing and put gauze between fingers and toes.
