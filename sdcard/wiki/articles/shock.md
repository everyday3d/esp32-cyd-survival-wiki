Shock happens when the body cannot get enough blood to the organs, often after bleeding, burns, severe pain or dehydration. It can kill even after the injury seems handled.

## Signs
- Pale, cool, clammy skin
- Fast, weak pulse and fast breathing
- Anxiety, restlessness or confusion
- Thirst, nausea
- Later: drowsiness and unresponsiveness

## What to do
1. Stop any bleeding.
2. Lay the person down on their back.
3. If there is no injury to the head, neck, back, hips or legs, you may raise the legs about 30 cm (12 in).
4. **Keep them warm.** Put insulation under them and cover them. Cold makes shock worse.
5. Keep them calm and reassure them.
6. Do not give food or drink if they may need surgery soon. In a remote setting where help is many hours away and the person is fully alert, small sips of water are reasonable.
7. Check breathing and pulse often. Start [CPR](cpr.md) if they stop breathing normally.

> If vomiting or not fully awake, turn them onto their side to protect the airway.
