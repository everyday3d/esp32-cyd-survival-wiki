## First: stay with the vehicle
A car is shelter and is easier for rescuers to spot than a person. **Do not walk away** unless help is clearly within a short, visible distance. Whiteouts make people lose their way within meters of the road.

## Make it easy to find you
- Turn on the hazard lights and call for help if you have a signal. Say exactly where you are.
- Tie a bright cloth to the antenna or a window. Raise the hood when the storm ends.
- Clear snow from the car roof to help aircraft see it. See [Helping Searchers Find You](helping-searchers.md).

## Keep warm
- **Run the engine about 10 minutes every hour** for heat. Crack a window on the side away from the wind.
- **Before each time you run the engine, clear snow from the tailpipe.** A blocked tailpipe fills the car with carbon monoxide. See [Carbon Monoxide](carbon-monoxide.md).
- Do not run the engine for long periods and do not fall into a deep sleep with it running. Take turns staying awake if you are with others.
- Wear every layer you have. Use floor mats, maps, seat covers and newspapers for insulation. Put on a hat. Huddle together.
- Move your arms and legs often. Stay dry.
- Keep fluids up. Do not eat snow, which cools you.

## Fuel
Only run the engine as much as needed, so fuel lasts. Turn it off in between and keep the dome light and radio use low to save the battery.

## Watch for cold injury
See [Hypothermia and Frostbite](hypothermia.md). Shivering that stops, confusion and drowsiness mean it is serious.

## Prevent it
Check forecasts, keep the fuel tank over half full in winter and carry a kit. See [Car Emergency Kit](car-kit.md).
