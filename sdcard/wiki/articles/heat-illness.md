## Heat exhaustion
**Signs:** heavy sweating, pale or flushed skin, weakness, dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps, fast pulse.

1. Move to shade and rest.
2. Loosen and remove extra clothing.
3. Cool with wet cloths and fanning.
4. Give water or a salt-and-sugar drink in small sips.
5. Rest for the rest of the day. If not better within an hour, treat as heat stroke.

## Heat stroke (life-threatening)
**Signs:** very hot body, **confusion, odd behavior, slurred speech, seizure or collapse**. Skin may be dry or still sweating.

> Any confusion in a hot, overheated person is heat stroke until proven otherwise. Cool first, transport second.

1. Cool them **immediately and aggressively**.
2. Best: put them in cool or cold water up to the neck (a stream, lake or tub), keeping the head above water and staying with them.
3. If you cannot: soak them with water and fan hard. Put cold packs or wet cloths on the neck, armpits and groin. Keep re-wetting.
4. Keep cooling until they are thinking clearly again.
5. Do not give drinks to someone who is confused or not fully awake.
6. Get medical help. Heat stroke can damage organs even after recovery.

## Prevention
Drink regularly, rest in the shade, work in the cool hours, and watch each other for early signs.
