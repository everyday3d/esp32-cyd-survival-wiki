Portable generators are a leading cause of carbon monoxide deaths after storms.

## Where to run it
- **Outdoors only**, never in a garage, basement, shed, porch or near an open window or door.
- Keep it at least **6 m (20 ft)** from the house, with the exhaust pointing away from any openings.
- Put it on level ground in a dry spot. Keep it dry with a generator tent or canopy open on the sides. Never run it in rain or standing water.
- Fit **battery CO alarms** in the house. See [Carbon Monoxide](carbon-monoxide.md).

## Connecting it
- Plug appliances **directly into the generator** or use a heavy-duty outdoor extension cord rated for the load. Check cords for damage.
- **Never plug a generator into a wall outlet.** This "backfeeding" can electrocute utility workers and neighbors and damage your house wiring.
- To power the house wiring, have an electrician install a **transfer switch**.
- Do not overload it. Add up the watts of what you plan to run, and start the biggest loads first.

## Fuel
- **Turn it off and let it cool** before refueling. Gasoline spilled on a hot engine can ignite.
- Store fuel in approved containers, outside, away from living spaces and ignition sources.
- Do not store more fuel than you need. Fuel goes stale in a few months. Use a stabilizer.

## Other tips
- Use the generator only when needed and in short, planned periods.
- Never touch it with wet hands.
- Run it for a few minutes every month and check the oil. See [Home Emergencies and Power Outages](power-outage.md).
