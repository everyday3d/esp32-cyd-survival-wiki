Children are not small adults. They get sick faster, hide it well, and can get worse quickly. Use the other first aid guides, plus the points below.

## Fast checks
- **Awake and responsive?** Calling their name, touching them and watching for movement.
- **Breathing:** count breaths for 30 seconds and double it. Normal at rest is roughly 30 to 60 a minute for a baby, 24 to 40 for a toddler and 18 to 30 for a school-age child. Much faster, labored or noisy breathing, or skin pulling in between the ribs, is serious.
- **Color:** blue, gray or very pale lips and skin are an emergency.
- **Drinking and wet diapers or urine:** a child who will not drink or has not passed urine in 8 hours needs help.

## Get help immediately for
- Trouble breathing, bluish color, grunting with each breath
- Unresponsive, very floppy, hard to wake, or seems confused
- A seizure that lasts more than 5 minutes, or a first seizure. See [Seizures](seizures.md).
- A baby under 3 months with a fever of 38 C (100.4 F) or higher. See [Fever in Children](child-fever.md).
- A head injury with vomiting, a severe headache, drowsiness or unequal pupils. See [Head Injuries and Concussion](head-injury.md).
- A stiff neck, or a rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass
- Swallowed medicine, batteries, magnets or chemicals. Call a poison center or emergency number at once.
- Signs of allergic reaction. See [Severe Allergic Reaction (Anaphylaxis)](anaphylaxis.md).

## Do not
- **Never give aspirin to a child or teen.** It can cause a dangerous illness.
- **Never give honey to a baby under 1 year.** It can cause botulism.
- Do not give cough and cold medicine to young children.
- Dose medicine by **weight, not age**, using the label and the measuring syringe or cup that came with it.
- **Never shake a baby**, even in frustration.

## CPR, choking and fluids
- CPR for a child and infant: see [CPR](cpr.md).
- Choking in a child or infant: see [Choking](choking.md). Never do abdominal thrusts on a baby under 1.
- Dehydration and rehydration drink: see [Diarrhea, Vomiting and Rehydration](diarrhea-vomiting.md).

## Be kind and honest
Get down to their level, speak calmly, tell them what you are doing and let them hold a toy or your hand. See [Helping Kids Cope](kids-calm.md).
