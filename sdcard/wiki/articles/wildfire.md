Wildfires can move faster than you can run and can throw embers a mile ahead of the flames.

## Get ready
- Keep a go-bag by the door with medicines, documents, water, a flashlight, a radio and an N95 mask.
- Know two routes out. Keep the car fueled and parked facing out.
- Clear dry leaves, pine needles and brush from around the house and gutters.

## If you are told to evacuate
- **Go early.** Do not wait to see the flames. Roads can fill or be blocked quickly.
- Close windows and doors but leave them unlocked. Turn off gas and propane if you can do so quickly.
- Take pets. Tell someone where you are going.

## If you are trapped in a vehicle
- Park in a clear area away from trees and brush, if you can, and not on the road.
- Close all windows and vents. Turn off the fan and air conditioning.
- Lie on the floor below the window line and cover yourself with a blanket or coat (wool is best).
- Stay inside. A car is a better shield than the open air. The fire front usually passes in minutes.
- Do not drive through heavy smoke or flames.

## If you are on foot
- Go to an area that has already burned, bare ground, a rock slide, or a body of water.
- Avoid steep slopes and canyons. Fire runs uphill fast.
- Lie face down on bare ground, cover your head and keep your mouth and nose covered with wet cloth.

## Smoke
- Smoke can hurt hours or days away from the fire. Stay inside with windows and doors closed.
- Use a **N95 or P100 respirator** if you must be out. Cloth and dust masks do not stop smoke particles.
- Run the air conditioner on recirculate, or a HEPA filter, if you have one.
- Seek care for chest pain, trouble breathing or severe coughing.
- Drink plenty of water and avoid hard exercise in smoky air.
