Most survival deaths come from bad decisions made in the first hour. When things go wrong, use **STOP**.

## S - Stop
Sit down. Drink some water, eat something small. Do not keep walking. Panic burns energy and makes you more lost.

## T - Think
- What happened, and what do you actually have?
- Does anyone know where you are and when you are due back?
- What is the biggest danger right now?

## O - Observe
- Weather: is it getting colder, darker, wetter?
- Terrain: water sources, shelter material, open areas for signaling.
- Your body: injuries, warmth, thirst, how much daylight is left.
- Your gear: empty your pockets and pack. Everything counts.

## P - Plan
Decide on one clear plan and follow it. Re-check it every few hours.

> If someone knows your route and expected return, it is usually best to **stay put**. Searchers find people who stay in one place far more often.

## Stay or go?
Stay if: people will look for you, you are injured, the weather is bad, or you do not know which way to go.

Move only if: no one will search for you, your location is unsafe, or you know a clear route to help. If you move, leave a note or arrow showing your direction.
