Carbon monoxide (CO) has no smell, color or taste. It comes from anything that burns fuel: furnaces, water heaters, stoves, fireplaces, gas heaters, generators, grills and cars. It builds up in closed spaces and can kill in minutes at high levels.

## Signs
- Headache (the most common), dizziness, weakness
- Nausea or vomiting, confusion
- Chest pain, blurred vision
- **Several people or pets sick at once**, or symptoms that get better when you leave the building.
- People who are asleep can die without any warning.

## If the alarm sounds or you suspect CO
1. **Get everyone out into fresh air at once**, including pets. Do not stop to open windows.
2. **Call 911** (or your local emergency number) from outside.
3. Count everyone. Do not go back in until firefighters say it is safe.
4. Get checked at a hospital even if you feel better. Tell them you may have had CO exposure.

## First aid
- Move the person to fresh air and keep them calm and warm.
- If they are not breathing, start CPR. See [CPR](cpr.md).
- A person who is unconscious, confused, pregnant, or a child needs urgent hospital care. They may need oxygen.

## Prevention
- **Put battery or plug-in CO alarms on every level and near sleeping areas.** Test them monthly and replace them when they expire (about every 5 to 7 years).
- **Never run a generator, grill, camp stove or heater inside** a house, garage, tent, camper or near an open window. See [Generator Safety](generator-safety.md).
- **Never run a car in an attached garage**, even with the door open.
- Have furnaces, chimneys and water heaters checked each year.
- After a storm or in winter, keep the tailpipe and exhaust vents clear of snow. See [Stranded in Your Car in Winter](stranded-winter.md).
