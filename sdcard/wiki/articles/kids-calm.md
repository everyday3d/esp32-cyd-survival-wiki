Children watch adults to decide how scared to be. Your calm is the best comfort you can give.

## In the moment
- **Stay calm and speak slowly.** Kneel to their level.
- Tell them the truth in simple words: *"There is a big storm. We are going to the basement where we will be safe. I am with you."*
- Tell them what happens next, then do it. Predictability is calming.
- Give them a small job: hold the flashlight, carry a teddy, pick the snacks. Feeling useful reduces fear.
- Keep them close. Hold hands, hold them, or sit with them. Touch calms.

## Comfort items
Keep a comfort object, favorite book, small toy, cards or coloring things in your go-bag. Games like "I spy" and singing help pass the time and distract.

## Routines
- Keep meals, naps and bedtime as close to normal as you can.
- Offer food and water often. Hungry, tired and thirsty kids are scared kids.

## Limit the news
Little ones do not need to see or hear frightening news and images. Turn off the TV or radio around them when you can.

## Listen
- Let them talk, draw or play out what they feel. Do not say "do not be scared." Say "It is okay to be scared. I am here."
- Answer questions simply and honestly. It is fine to say, "I do not know, but I will find out."
- Do not promise what you cannot control. Say "We will do everything we can to stay safe."

## Signs they are struggling
- Clinging, crying, tantrums, going back to earlier behaviors (bed-wetting, thumb-sucking)
- Trouble sleeping, nightmares, headaches, stomachaches
- Fear of being alone, or of the sound of wind, thunder or sirens
- Withdrawal, anger, or no interest in play

These are common and usually settle over days to weeks with comfort and routine. If they last, or you are worried, ask a doctor, school counselor or crisis line.

## Look after yourself too
You cannot pour from an empty cup. Eat, drink, rest when you can, and ask other adults to take a turn.
