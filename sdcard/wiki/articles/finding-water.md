## Where to look
- **Follow the land downhill.** Water collects in valleys, gullies and low ground.
- **Green vegetation** such as willows, cottonwoods, reeds and cattails usually means water is near or just under the surface.
- **Animal trails** often lead to water, especially where several trails join and head downhill.
- **Birds** circling or flying low and straight at dawn and dusk are often heading to water.
- **Insects:** bees and flies usually stay within a few kilometers of water.
- **Dry stream beds:** dig on the outside of bends, where the last water would sit. If the sand is damp, dig a hole and wait for water to seep in.

## Collecting water
- **Rain:** spread a tarp, poncho or plastic sheet and funnel it into a container. Rainwater is the cleanest natural source.
- **Dew:** at dawn, wipe grass with a cloth and wring it out.
- **Snow and ice:** melt it first. Eating snow lowers your body temperature. Ice gives more water per pot than snow.
- **Transpiration bag:** tie a clear plastic bag over a leafy, non-poisonous branch in the sun. Water condenses inside.

> Treat all water from the ground, streams, lakes, snow and dew before drinking. Even clear mountain water can carry parasites.

## Water to avoid
- Seawater and urine: they dehydrate you faster.
- Water near mines, factories, farm runoff, or with dead animals nearby.
- Water with oily film, strong smell or no living plants around it.
