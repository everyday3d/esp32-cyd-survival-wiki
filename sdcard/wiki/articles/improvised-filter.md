A layered filter removes mud, debris and some taste. It does **not** make water safe on its own. Always boil or disinfect after filtering.

![Layers of an improvised filter](img/water_filter.jpg)

## What you need
- A container with a small hole in the bottom: plastic bottle with the bottom cut off, a bucket, or a cloth tied into a cone
- Cloth or grass
- Fine sand
- Charcoal from your fire (not ash), crushed into small pieces
- Small gravel or pebbles

## Building it
1. If using a bottle, cut off the bottom and turn it upside down. Plug the neck loosely with cloth.
2. Add layers from the bottom up: cloth, small gravel, fine sand, crushed charcoal, fine sand, then cloth or grass on top.
3. Make each layer several centimeters thick. More sand and charcoal means clearer water.
4. Pour water in slowly. Throw away the first batch; it will carry dust from the charcoal.
5. Run cloudy water through two or three times if needed.

> Charcoal from a campfire is not the same as activated charcoal. It helps with taste and color, but it does not remove chemicals or germs reliably.

## After filtering
Boil, use bleach, or use tablets. See [Making Water Safe to Drink](purifying-water.md).
