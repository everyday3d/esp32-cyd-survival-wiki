## Make it safe first
1. **Do not touch the person** while they may still be in contact with power.
2. Turn the power off at the switch, breaker or meter if you can reach it safely. Unplug the cord if the plug is dry and undamaged.
3. If you cannot cut the power, push the source away with something **dry and not metal**, such as a wooden broom handle or a dry rope. Stand on something dry.
4. **Downed power lines:** stay at least 10 m (33 ft) away. Ground near a line can carry current. Shuffle away with feet together if you feel a tingle. Call the power company and emergency services.

## Then
1. Check if they respond and are breathing. Call emergency services.
2. **Not breathing:** start CPR.
3. Cool burns with running water for 10-20 minutes. Cover them loosely with clean, non-stick cloth. Look for a second burn where the current left the body.
4. Lay them down, keep them warm and treat for shock. Do not move them if you suspect a neck or back injury.

## Get medical help for everyone who was shocked
- The heart can be affected even if they feel fine. Anyone with high voltage, a long contact, burns, faintness, chest pain or an irregular pulse needs a hospital.

## Lightning
- A person struck by lightning carries no charge. It is safe to touch them.
- Many strikes stop the heart or breathing, and CPR works. If several people are hurt, treat those who are not breathing first.

> Never touch a car that has a power line on it. Stay inside unless it is on fire, then jump clear with both feet together and shuffle away.
