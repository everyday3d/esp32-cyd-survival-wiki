A battery that clicks or turns slowly and weakly is usually just flat. Check your car manual first. Some modern cars have special jump points.

## Before you start
- Do **not** try if the battery is cracked, leaking, bulging or frozen.
- Switch both cars off and take the keys out. Turn off lights and radio.
- No smoking, flames or sparks near batteries. They give off explosive gas.
- Park the cars close but **not touching**.

## Connect the cables in this order
1. **Red** clamp to the **positive (+)** terminal of the **dead** battery.
2. Other **red** clamp to the **positive (+)** terminal of the **good** battery.
3. **Black** clamp to the **negative (-)** terminal of the **good** battery.
4. Last **black** clamp to **bare, unpainted metal** on the dead car's engine block or frame, **away from the battery**. This is the ground. It may spark a little; that is why it goes away from the battery.

Make sure the clamps do not touch each other.

## Start
1. Start the **good** car and let it run a few minutes.
2. Try to start the dead car. If it does not start, wait a few more minutes and try again. Do not crank for more than about 10 seconds at a time.
3. Once it starts, leave it running.

## Remove the cables in reverse order
Black from the ground, black from the good battery, red from the good battery, red from the dead battery.

## After
- Drive for at least 20 to 30 minutes to recharge the battery. Do not turn the engine off during this time.
- If it keeps dying, the battery or alternator may be failing. Get it tested.
- In the cold, a battery loses power. A portable jump pack is a good addition to your kit. See [Car Emergency Kit](car-kit.md).
