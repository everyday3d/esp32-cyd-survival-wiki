A car fire can spread in under a minute. Plastics, fuel and tires burn hot and fast. **People matter more than the car.**

## If you see smoke or flames
1. **Pull over** to a safe spot as soon as you can, away from buildings and dry grass. Turn on the hazard lights.
2. **Turn off the engine.** This stops fuel and spark.
3. **Get everyone out.** Take children and pets. Leave your belongings.
4. **Move at least 30 m (100 ft) away**, upwind and away from traffic. Tires, fuel tanks and shocks can burst or explode.
5. **Call emergency services** with your exact location.
6. Keep everyone back, away from the road.

## Do not
- **Do not open the hood.** The sudden rush of air can make the fire flare.
- Do not go back for belongings.
- Do not use a small extinguisher on anything larger than a very small fire. If you have one and the fire is tiny and not near the fuel or passenger area, you may use it from a safe distance. Otherwise, leave.

## If you cannot get out
- If your seat belt is jammed, cut it with a seat belt cutter or knife if you have one.
- Use a window-breaker tool on a side window (not the windshield). See [Car in Water](car-in-water.md).

## If your clothing catches fire
**Stop, drop and roll.** Cover your face with your hands and roll to put it out. See [Burns](burns.md).

## Prevent it
- Fix fuel and oil leaks and frayed wiring.
- Carry a small fire extinguisher rated for car use in the passenger compartment, not the trunk.
- Do not leave lighters, aerosol cans or batteries in a hot car.
