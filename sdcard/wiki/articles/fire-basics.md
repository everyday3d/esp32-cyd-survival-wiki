Fire gives warmth, light, safe water, cooked food, a signal and a big morale boost. Make it before you need it badly.

![The fire triangle](img/fire_triangle.jpg)

## What a fire needs
- **Heat** to start it.
- **Fuel** to burn.
- **Oxygen.** Do not pack wood too tightly. A fire that is smothered goes out.

## Three sizes of fuel
![Tinder, kindling and fuel](img/fire_materials.jpg)

- **Tinder** catches a spark or flame: dry grass, birch bark, cedar bark shredded fine, pine needles, cattail fluff, cotton, paper, wood shavings.
- **Kindling** carries the flame: dry twigs from match to pencil thickness.
- **Fuel** keeps it going: sticks and logs from thumb to wrist thickness, then larger.

> Gather all three sizes **before** you strike a light. A new fire dies in the time it takes to go looking for more sticks.

## Finding dry wood in wet weather
- Snap test: dry wood breaks with a clean crack. If it bends, it is too wet.
- Take dead branches still on standing trees, not from the ground.
- Look under thick evergreens, inside rotten stumps and under overhangs.
- Split wet sticks; the center is often dry.
- Shave thin curls (feather sticks) from dry wood for kindling.
- Resin-rich pine wood (fatwood) burns even when damp.

## Building up
Light the tinder, then add kindling a few pieces at a time. Add larger fuel only when the smaller sizes are burning strongly. Blow gently at the base if it struggles.
