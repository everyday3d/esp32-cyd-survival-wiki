A cold night is miserable and dangerous, and it wrecks your judgment the next day. These tricks work with or without good gear.

## Before bed
- **Eat** something, especially fats and sugars. Your body burns food to make heat. Keep a snack by your bed for the middle of the night.
- **Drink** warm water. Being dehydrated makes you colder.
- **Pee** before you lie down. Your body wastes heat keeping a full bladder warm.
- **Warm up** with light exercise (not to the point of sweating) right before getting in.
- **Change into dry clothes**, especially socks. Put damp clothes in your bag to dry them with body heat if you have no other way.

## Under you matters most
The ground pulls heat from you faster than the air. Put down **at least 10-20 cm (4-8 in)** of dry insulation: leaves, grass, pine boughs, a foam pad, an empty pack, a coiled rope. Then more.

## Around you
- Wear a **hat** and cover your neck.
- Fill air gaps in your sleeping bag or blankets with spare clothes.
- Fill a bottle with hot water (check the lid seals and the bottle can take heat), wrap it in a sock, and put it between your thighs or on your stomach.
- Keep your face out of the bag; breath adds moisture that makes it colder.
- Huddle together with others if you can.

## Through the night
- If you wake up cold, eat, do some sit-ups or clench and relax your muscles, then try again.
- Keep tomorrow's water and boots inside your shelter or bag so they do not freeze.

> Shivering is a warning. If someone shivers hard and cannot stop, or starts stumbling and mumbling, treat for [Hypothermia](hypothermia.md) now.
