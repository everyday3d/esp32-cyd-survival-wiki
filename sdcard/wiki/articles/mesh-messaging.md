This device can send short text messages to other Survival Wiki boards nearby, with **no internet, phone signal or router**.

## How it works
- Boards talk to each other directly using a short-range radio (ESP-NOW, on the same 2.4 GHz band as Wi-Fi).
- **Every board relays messages it hears.** A message can hop through several boards, so more boards means more range.
- Messages are short, up to about 64 characters.
- The sender sees **"Heard by"**: which boards received the message.

## Using it on the board
1. Tap **Mesh** on the home screen.
2. Make sure mesh is turned on, then pick a quick message or type your own.
3. Watch for replies. A star on the Mesh button means you have unread messages.
4. The **Nearby** screen shows boards that are around you and a rough distance.

## Using it from your phone
1. Turn on the board's Wi-Fi hotspot and join it with your phone.
2. Open the chat page. Type longer messages on your phone and the board sends them over the mesh.
3. The phone also gets maps and the wiki from the board.

## Range
- A few hundred meters between two boards in the open is typical. Buildings, hills and trees cut that sharply.
- Raise the board up high, and keep it away from metal and your body.
- Space your group out, but keep every board within reach of at least one other.

## Tips
- Agree on a short check-in time so batteries last: for example, 10 minutes past each hour.
- Keep messages short and clear. Say where you are and what you need.
- It is a **short-range text tool**, not a replacement for a phone or a radio call for help. For wide-area help, see [Choosing an Emergency Radio](radio-basics.md) and [Radio and Phone Tips](emergency-radio.md).
- Power matters: a USB power bank can run a board for many hours.
