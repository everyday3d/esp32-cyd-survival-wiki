A fallen line can still be live. It does not have to spark or buzz to be deadly. The ground around it can also carry current.

## If you see a downed line
- **Stay at least 10 m (33 ft) away**, about the length of a bus, and keep other people back too.
- Do not touch the line, or anything touching it: trees, fences, puddles, vehicles, branches.
- **Call the power company and 911.** Keep watching until help arrives so no one walks into it.
- Do not try to move a line with a stick or tool.
- Treat every line as live, including cable, phone and "dead-looking" lines. They may be touching a live power line.

## If a line falls on your car
- **Stay inside.** Honk the horn or call for help and wait for the utility to turn the power off.
- Warn others to stay away.
- **Only get out if the car is on fire.** In that case:
  1. Open the door and **jump clear** with both feet together, without touching the car and ground at the same time.
  2. **Shuffle away** with your feet together and touching the ground, in small steps. Do not take long strides. The ground near the line has different voltages, and a long step can shock you.
  3. Keep going until you are at least 10 m (33 ft) away.

## If you are near a line and feel a tingle
Your feet are in a danger area. **Shuffle away** with feet together, in small steps.

## After storms and floods
- Do not drive through or walk into water near a downed line.
- Report downed lines even if no one is hurt.
- Do not restore power at home if there is damage or flooding. See [Utility Shutoffs](utility-shutoffs.md).

## First aid
If someone is shocked, **do not touch them** until the power is off. Then call for help, check breathing and begin CPR if needed. See [CPR](cpr.md) and [Burns](burns.md).
