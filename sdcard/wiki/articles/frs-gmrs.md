FRS and GMRS handhelds share the same 22 channels around 462 and 467 MHz. FRS radios need no license. GMRS has more power and repeaters and needs a license in the US.

## Using them
1. Turn on both radios and set the **same channel**.
2. Set the privacy code (sometimes called CTCSS or "sub-channel") to **0 or off** so you can hear everyone. Codes only filter what you hear. They do not make a call private.
3. **Press and hold** the push-to-talk button, wait a second, speak in a normal voice with the radio a few centimeters from your mouth, then release.
4. Only one person can talk at a time. If you are talking, you cannot hear.

## Channels
- **Channels 1 to 7 and 15 to 22** are the stronger channels. Channels 8 to 14 are low power, mainly for short-range use.
- **Channel 9** is widely used as an emergency and calling channel, and is monitored by some volunteer groups. Many families agree on a different channel for themselves.
- Everyone nearby can hear you. Do not give out personal details.

## Range
- Usually 1 to 3 km (0.5 to 2 miles) in towns and woods. In open country, or from a hilltop, it can be much farther.
- Buildings, hills and dense trees cut range a lot. See [Getting More Range](radio-range.md).
- Packaging claims of "30 miles" are not realistic.

## GMRS extras
- Higher power and detachable antennas for much better range.
- **Repeaters** on channels 15R to 22R can cover a whole town. Repeater access needs a privacy code the repeater owner gives you.
- Check your local rules. In the US, a GMRS license covers your whole family.

## Tips
- Use short, clear messages. See [Making an Emergency Radio Call](radio-calls.md).
- Agree on a check-in time. Turn radios on only at set times to save batteries.
- Keep spare batteries or a charger. Rechargeable radios need a way to charge.
- A car charger or power bank keeps radios alive when the power is out.
