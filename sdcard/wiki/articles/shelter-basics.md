In cold, wet or windy weather, shelter is your top priority after first aid. A good shelter is small, dry, out of the wind and insulated from the ground.

## How you lose heat
- **Conduction:** touching cold ground or rock. The ground steals heat fastest, so insulate under you first.
- **Convection:** wind blowing heat away.
- **Evaporation:** wet clothes and sweat.
- **Radiation:** heat leaving your body to the open sky.

## Choosing a site
- Flat, slightly raised and well-drained ground.
- Near building materials, firewood and water, but at least 60 m (200 ft) from streams.
- Out of the wind: behind rocks, thick trees or a ridge.

## Avoid
- **Dead trees and hanging branches** ("widowmakers") above you.
- Dry stream beds and valley floors (floods, and cold air sinks there at night).
- Bases of cliffs (rockfall) and avalanche slopes.
- Insect nests and animal trails.

> Pile at least 10-20 cm (4-8 in) of dry leaves, grass, pine boughs or a pack between you and the ground, even inside a tent. It compresses when you lie on it.

## Before dark
Start your shelter at least two hours before sunset. Building in the dark is slow and dangerous.
