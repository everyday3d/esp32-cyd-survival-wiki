Natural gas and propane can explode from a tiny spark. A gas company adds a smell, like **rotten eggs or sulfur**, so you notice a leak.

## If you smell gas or hear hissing
1. **Do not** use light switches, appliances, garage door openers, lighters, matches or a phone indoors. Any spark can ignite gas.
2. **Do not** start a car in an attached garage.
3. **Get everyone out** right now, including pets. Leave doors open behind you if you can do so quickly.
4. **Move well away** from the building, upwind.
5. **Call the gas company's emergency number and 911 from outside** or from a neighbor's house.
6. **Do not go back in** until the gas company or fire department says it is safe.

## Shutting off the gas
- Only do this if you can reach the main valve safely and **without going into a gassy area**.
- Use a wrench. Turn the valve a quarter turn so the handle is **crossways** to the pipe.
- Once it is off, **do not turn it back on yourself.** The gas company must restore service and relight pilot lights.
- For propane tanks, close the valve on the tank by turning it clockwise.

## Propane
Propane is heavier than air. It sinks and collects in basements and low areas, so it can linger even with doors open.

## Signs of a leak
- A sulfur or rotten egg smell, or a hissing or whistling sound near a gas line.
- Dead or dying plants near a buried gas line, or bubbles in standing water.
- Headaches, dizziness, nausea or fatigue indoors with no other cause. See [Carbon Monoxide](carbon-monoxide.md).

## After
Do not use the appliance or relight anything until a professional inspects it.
