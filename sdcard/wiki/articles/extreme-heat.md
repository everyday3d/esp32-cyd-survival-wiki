Heat kills more people than most other weather. Older adults, small children, outdoor workers and people with health problems are at the highest risk.

## Stay cool
- Stay in air conditioning if you can. A few hours a day in a cool place (a library, mall or cooling center) helps your body.
- Without AC, stay on the lowest floor out of the sun. Close curtains and blinds on sunny windows.
- Take cool showers or baths. Wet your skin, hair and clothes and sit in front of a fan.
- A fan alone does not cool you when air is above about 35 C (95 F). Use water on your skin too.
- Wear light, loose, light-colored clothing and a wide hat.
- **Do hard work in the cool early morning or evening.**

## Drink
- Drink water before you feel thirsty. See [Dehydration and Water Needs](dehydration.md).
- Avoid alcohol and large amounts of sugary drinks.
- If you sweat a lot, replace salt too, with food or an oral rehydration drink.

## Never leave anyone in a parked car
Inside temperatures can reach deadly levels in minutes, even with the windows cracked. This applies to **children and pets**. Look in the back seat every time you leave the car.

## Check on others
Call or visit older neighbors and people who live alone at least twice a day.

## Know the warning signs
- **Heat exhaustion:** heavy sweating, cold, clammy skin, weakness, headache, nausea.
- **Heat stroke is an emergency:** hot, red, dry or damp skin, confusion, fainting, a body temperature above 40 C (104 F). **Call for help and cool the person right away.** See [Heat Exhaustion and Heat Stroke](heat-illness.md).

## If the power fails
Move to the coolest part of the house or a cooling center. Use wet cloths on the neck, armpits and groin. See [Desert and Hot Weather Shelter](hot-weather.md).
