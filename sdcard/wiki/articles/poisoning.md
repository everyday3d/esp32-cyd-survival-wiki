## First
1. **Get the person and yourself away from the source.** Open windows and get fresh air for fumes.
2. Check if they respond and are breathing. Start CPR if they are not.
3. **Call poison control or emergency services.** In the US, Poison Control is 1-800-222-1222. Have ready: who, how old, how much, what and when.
4. Keep the container, pill bottle or plant sample to show the responders.

## What to do
- **Swallowed:** if they are awake and alert, rinse the mouth. Give sips of water only if a poison expert says so.
- **On skin:** remove contaminated clothing, rinse with plenty of running water for 15-20 minutes.
- **In eyes:** hold the eyelid open and rinse with clean water for at least 15 minutes.
- **Fumes or gas:** move them into fresh air. See also the carbon monoxide guide.

## What not to do
- **Do not make them vomit.** It can burn the throat a second time or cause choking.
- Do not give salt water, milk, or home remedies.
- Do not wait for symptoms to appear.

## Opioid overdose
- Signs: very slow or stopped breathing, blue lips, pinpoint pupils, cannot wake them.
- Give **naloxone (Narcan)** if you have it, call emergency services and give rescue breaths or CPR. A second dose after 2-3 minutes is safe if there is no response.
- Place them on their side if breathing. Stay until help arrives.

## Recovery position
- If they are unresponsive but breathing, roll them onto their side and watch their breathing.

> Children are poisoned most often by medicine, cleaners and button batteries. Keep them locked away. A swallowed button battery or magnet is an emergency even with no symptoms.
