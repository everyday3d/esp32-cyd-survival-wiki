Carry at least two ways to make fire. Friction methods are a last resort, not a plan.

## Lighter and matches
- A cheap disposable lighter is the most reliable tool. Keep one in a warm pocket; cold lighters fail.
- Waterproof your matches in a sealed container. Strike away from your body and shield the flame with your hands.

## Ferro rod (fire steel)
1. Place the rod tip right in the tinder.
2. Hold the striker still and pull the rod back hard, so sparks fall into the tinder without knocking it apart.
3. Use very fine, dry tinder: cotton with petroleum jelly, cattail fluff, fine bark shavings.

## Battery and steel wool
Touch both terminals of a 9-volt battery to fine steel wool, or bridge a flashlight battery with a strand. The wool glows and lights tinder. Keep steel wool away from batteries when packing.

## Lens
A magnifying glass, eyeglasses for far-sightedness, or a clear bottle of water can focus sunlight onto dark tinder. Needs bright sun.

## Bow drill (last resort)
1. **Fireboard:** dry, soft, dead wood (willow, cedar, poplar, basswood) about 2 cm thick.
2. **Spindle:** straight, dry stick of the same wood, thumb-thick, about 20 cm long.
3. **Bearing block:** a hard piece of wood or stone with a dent, to press down on the spindle.
4. **Bow:** a curved stick about arm's length with cord tied loosely so you can twist the spindle into it.
5. Cut a notch from the edge of the fireboard to a small burned-in hole. Spin the spindle fast with long, steady bow strokes while pressing down.
6. When smoking dust fills the notch and keeps smoking on its own, you have an ember. Tip it into a tinder bundle and blow gently until it flames.

> Friction fire takes practice and a lot of energy. Learn it before you need it.
