## Insects
Insects are high in protein and fat and easy to catch.
- **Good choices:** grasshoppers, crickets, ants, termites, beetle grubs (in rotten logs), earthworms.
- **Avoid:** brightly colored insects, hairy caterpillars, anything that stings or bites (except ants once cooked), flies, ticks and mosquitoes.
- Cook them: roast or boil. Remove legs and wings from grasshoppers. Cooking kills parasites.

## Fishing
- **Improvised hooks:** bent safety pins, wire, thorns, carved bone or hardwood.
- **Line:** fishing line from your kit, inner strands of paracord, dental floss.
- **Bait:** insects, worms, small pieces of fish guts.
- Fish in the early morning and evening, in shade, near logs and in deeper pools below rapids.
- Set several lines at once and check them often, rather than holding one rod.
- A **fish trap**: a ring of stones in shallow water with a narrow funnel entrance. Fish swim in and struggle to find their way out.

> Cook all freshwater fish thoroughly. Avoid fish that look sick, smell bad, or have slimy gills or sunken eyes.

## Shellfish
- Freshwater mussels and crayfish are good, cooked well.
- Seashore shellfish can carry toxins from algae blooms (red tide), which cooking does not destroy. Avoid during warnings or in warm months if unsure.

## Small game
Snares and traps work, but take skill and time, and are illegal in many places outside emergencies. Check many traps over a wide area. Always cook wild meat until it is no longer pink.
