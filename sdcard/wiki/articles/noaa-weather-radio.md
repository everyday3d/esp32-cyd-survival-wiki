## NOAA Weather Radio (US)
- A network of stations broadcasting weather, hazard and civil alerts, 24 hours a day.
- Seven channels between **162.400 and 162.550 MHz**. Your radio finds the strongest. Many hand-crank radios include a weather band.
- Choose a radio with **SAME** (Specific Area Message Encoding). You enter your county code and it only sounds for your area.
- Set it to alert mode so it wakes you for warnings, even overnight.
- Keep spare batteries or a hand crank. It works when phone and internet do not.

## Other ways to hear alerts
- **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA)** on phones: short, loud messages for tornadoes, floods, evacuation orders and missing children. Keep them turned on.
- **AM/FM radio:** local stations carry Emergency Alert System messages. A battery or hand-crank radio is the dependable way to hear them. In a long disaster, many areas have one station that stays on air.
- **TV and weather apps:** useful if you still have power and data.
- **Shortwave** or **foreign broadcasts** may carry news if local stations are down.

## What the alert words mean
- **Watch:** conditions are right for it to happen. Be ready.
- **Warning:** it is happening or about to. **Act now.**
- **Advisory:** be aware and take care.

## What to do when an alert sounds
- Do not ignore it just because the sky looks clear.
- Follow the exact instructions: shelter, evacuate or stay inside.
- See the guides for the hazard: [Tornado Safety](tornado.md), [Floods and Flash Floods](flood.md), [Hurricanes and Storm Surge](hurricane.md), [Wildfire and Smoke](wildfire.md).
- Tell your neighbors, especially older people and those with no phone.

## Tips
- Test your radio monthly. Write the frequency list in your kit.
- Buy a model with a hand crank, solar panel and USB output, so it can charge your phone a little.
