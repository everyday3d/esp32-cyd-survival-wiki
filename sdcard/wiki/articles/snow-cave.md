## Why snow
Snow is a good insulator. Inside a snow shelter it can be around 0 C (32 F) while the wind chill outside is far below. Pick a spot out of the wind, away from avalanche slopes.

## Snow cave (needs a deep drift)
1. Find a drift **at least 2 m (6-7 ft) deep**, packed firm.
2. Dig a tunnel in, then upward to a sleeping platform higher than the entrance. Cold air sinks and warm air stays.
3. Make the roof a smooth dome, **at least 30 cm (1 ft) thick**, so it doesn't drip or collapse.
4. Poke a **ventilation hole** the size of a fist through the roof. Keep it open.
5. Block most of the doorway with a pack or blocks of snow. Keep a shovel inside.
6. Mark the outside with a pole or skis so rescuers can find it.

## Quinzee (when there is no drift)
1. Pile up snow about 2 m (6 ft) high and 3 m (10 ft) wide. Let it settle for an hour or two.
2. Push sticks about 30 cm (1 ft) long into the pile as thickness guides.
3. Hollow it out from a doorway, stopping when you reach the sticks. Smooth the ceiling.
4. Add a vent hole and block the door loosely.

## Staying safe
- **Keep the vent clear.** Check it often. Wet snow, a snowfall or a candle's use can block it. Poor air kills.
- Do not use a stove or burn fuel inside without good ventilation. Fumes and carbon monoxide.
- Dig carefully and stay dry. Take off outer layers while you work so you don't sweat. Put them back on before you rest.
- Put insulation under you: branches, packs, ropes, a pad.
- Keep your tools inside so you can dig out.
- A single candle can warm a small cave, but keep the vent open.

> Building takes 1-2 hours of hard work. Start before dark and before you are exhausted.
