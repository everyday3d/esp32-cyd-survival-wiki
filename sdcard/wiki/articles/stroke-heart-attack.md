## Stroke: think FAST
- **F**ace: one side droops or is numb when they smile.
- **A**rms: one arm drifts down or is weak when both are raised.
- **S**peech: slurred, strange, or they cannot understand you.
- **T**ime: note when it started and **call emergency services now**.

Other signs: sudden confusion, trouble seeing, dizziness or loss of balance, a sudden severe headache.

**Do:**
- Sit or lay them down with the head slightly raised. Keep them calm.
- **Note the time symptoms began.** Treatment depends on it.
- Loosen tight clothing. If they are unconscious but breathing, place them on their side.

**Do not:** give food, drink or pills (they may not swallow safely), and do not let them walk or drive.

## Heart attack
**Signs:**
- Pressure, squeezing or pain in the chest that may spread to the arm, jaw, neck or back
- Shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea, lightheadedness
- In women, older adults and diabetics, the signs can be milder: unusual tiredness, nausea, back or jaw pain

**Do:**
1. **Call emergency services.** Do not wait to see if it passes.
2. Have them sit and rest in a comfortable position, often half upright.
3. **Aspirin:** if they are not allergic and are awake, have them **chew one adult aspirin (325 mg)**, or 2 low-dose (81 mg) tablets. Do not give it if they have bleeding problems or have been told not to take it.
4. If prescribed nitroglycerin, help them take their own.
5. **If they collapse and stop breathing normally, start CPR.** Use an AED if one is available.

> Do not drive them yourself if an ambulance is possible. Responders can start treatment on the way.
