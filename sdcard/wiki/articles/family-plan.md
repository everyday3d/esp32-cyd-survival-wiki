Make the plan together, write it down and practice it. Kids who know what to do are calmer and safer.

## Choose
- **Two meeting places:** one near home (a tree, a neighbor's porch) and one outside your area (a library, a relative's house).
- **An out-of-area contact** everyone calls or texts when separated. Long-distance texts often get through when local calls do not.
- **A family code word** that only trusted people know. A kid should not go with anyone who cannot say it.

## Teach kids
- **Their full name, your name and your phone number.** Practice it like a song.
- **How to call 911** (or your local emergency number), and what to say: their name, where they are and what is wrong.
- How to stay put and wait for a trusted adult if they get separated.
- That in a fire they must never hide: get out and stay out. See [House Fire: Get Out](house-fire.md).
- To find a safe stranger in a crowd if lost: a person in uniform, someone with kids, or a store worker.

## Contact cards
- Put a **card with your names, numbers, the out-of-area contact, allergies and medicines** in each child's backpack and pocket.
- For little ones, write a number in permanent marker on their arm or sew a tag in their clothes, if you are separated.

## School and childcare
- Learn their **emergency and reunification plan**: where kids go and how you pick them up.
- Keep your contact details up to date, and name backup adults who may collect your child.
- Do not rush to the school unless told to. Roads must stay clear for buses and responders.

## Go-bag for kids
- Water, snacks, a change of clothes, a warm layer and shoes
- Comfort item, book, cards, coloring supplies
- Medicines, copies of prescriptions and a doctor's number
- For babies: diapers, formula, bottles. See [Caring for a Baby Without Power](baby-care.md).
- Flashlight and a whistle

## Practice
Twice a year, run a drill: get out of the house in the dark, meet at your meeting place, make a call. Discuss what went well.
