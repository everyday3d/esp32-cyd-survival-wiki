## How much daylight is left?
![Estimating time until sunset with your hand](img/hand_sunset.jpg)

1. Stretch your arm out fully, palm toward you, fingers together and level.
2. Put your bottom finger on the horizon.
3. Count how many finger-widths fit between the horizon and the sun.
4. Each finger is roughly **15 minutes** of daylight; four fingers is about an hour.

This is a rough guide that changes with your location and the season. Leave plenty of extra time. After sunset, twilight gives about 20-30 more minutes of usable light in most places.

## Sun basics
- The sun rises roughly in the **east** and sets roughly in the **west**.
- North of the tropics, the midday sun is in the **south**. South of the tropics, it is in the **north**.
- Sunrise and sunset are only exactly east and west near the equinoxes (March and September). In summer they shift toward the pole; in winter, toward the equator.

## Clues that are often right (Northern Hemisphere)
Reverse north and south in the Southern Hemisphere.
- **Snow** melts first on south-facing slopes and lasts longer on north-facing ones.
- South-facing slopes are often drier, with more open, sun-loving plants. North-facing slopes are often damper with denser growth.
- **Prevailing winds** shape trees and snowdrifts. If you know the usual wind direction in your area, bent trees and drift shapes point the way.
- **Churches:** older churches in Europe often have the altar at the east end.
- **Satellite dishes** in North America and Europe tend to face southward.

## Myths - do not rely on them
- **"Moss grows on the north side of trees."** Moss grows wherever it is damp and shady, which can be any side.
- **"Ant hills are on the south side."** Not reliable.
- **Tree rings wider on one side** depend on slope, light and wind, not direction.

> Combine several clues and confirm with the [Shadow-Stick Method](shadow-stick.md) or the stars before committing to a direction.
